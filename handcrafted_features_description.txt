COMPREHENSIVE GAIT FEATURES FOR PARKINSON'S DISEASE DETECTION
================================================================================

This document describes all 85 handcrafted gait features and their significance
in distinguishing Parkinson's disease from healthy controls.

FEATURE CATEGORIES:
1. Temporal Gait Features (Basic Timing)
2. Nonlinear Dynamics Features (Critical for PD)
3. Gait Phase Features  
4. Force Production Features
5. Signal Quality & Smoothness
6. Bilateral Asymmetry Features

================================================================================
1. TEMPORAL GAIT FEATURES (Basic Timing)
================================================================================

L_stride_time_mean
------------------
Description: Average time between consecutive left foot heel strikes
Normal Range: 1.0-1.2 seconds in healthy adults
PD Distinction: Parkinson's patients typically have SHORTER stride times due to:
- Bradykinesia (slowness of movement)
- Shuffling gait pattern
- Reduced step length
Why: Dopamine depletion affects basal ganglia timing control, leading to hurried, 
     shortened steps as a compensatory mechanism for instability.

L_stride_time_std
-----------------
Description: Standard deviation of left stride times
Normal: Low variability (0.02-0.08s)
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability in PD due to:
- Irregular stepping rhythm
- Loss of automatic movement control
- Freezing episodes
Why: Basal ganglia dysfunction disrupts internal timing mechanisms, causing 
     unpredictable stride timing.

L_stride_time_cv
----------------
Description: Coefficient of variation (std/mean) for left stride time
Normal: 2-8% in healthy gait
PD Distinction: SIGNIFICANTLY INCREASED (10-25%) in PD because:
- Greater proportional variability relative to mean stride time
- Inconsistent motor output
Why: Loss of dopamine-mediated motor rhythm generation in substantia nigra.

R_stride_time_mean, R_stride_time_std, R_stride_time_cv
--------------------------------------------------------
Description: Same as left features but for right foot
PD Distinction: Similar patterns as left side, but may show ASYMMETRY in 
                early-stage PD (hemiparkinsonism)

================================================================================
2. NONLINEAR DYNAMICS FEATURES (Most Critical for PD Detection)
================================================================================

L_dfa_alpha (Detrended Fluctuation Analysis)
--------------------------------------------
Description: Fractal scaling exponent measuring long-range correlations in stride intervals
Normal Range: 0.8-1.0 (healthy fractal gait)
PD Distinction: DECREASED α < 0.8 indicates:
- Loss of complex, adaptive gait patterns
- More rigid, stereotyped walking
- Reduced capacity for stride-to-stride adjustments
Why: Healthy gait exhibits fractal properties (self-similar patterns across time scales)
     due to flexible neural control. PD disrupts this, creating more random or 
     overly regular patterns.

R_dfa_alpha
-----------
Description: Right foot fractal scaling
PD Distinction: Same pattern as left, but asymmetry may occur in unilateral PD

L_poincare_SD1 (Poincaré Plot Short-term Variability)
-----------------------------------------------------
Description: Instantaneous stride-to-stride adjustments
Normal: Represents healthy automatic gait adjustments
PD Distinction: DECREASED SD1 indicates:
- Reduced ability to make fine step-to-step corrections
- Loss of gait automaticity
- Increased reliance on conscious control
Why: Basal ganglia normally provide automatic movement adjustments. PD patients
     must consciously control each step, reducing natural variability.

L_poincare_SD2 (Poincaré Plot Long-term Variability)
----------------------------------------------------
Description: Overall stride pattern variability across multiple steps
PD Distinction: May be DECREASED or altered, indicating:
- Loss of adaptive capacity over longer sequences
- Reduced response to environmental changes
Why: Inability to modulate gait pattern based on changing conditions or fatigue.

L_poincare_SD_ratio (SD1/SD2)
------------------------------
Description: Balance between short-term and long-term variability
PD Distinction: ALTERED ratio indicates imbalance in:
- Automatic vs voluntary control mechanisms
- Immediate corrections vs overall pattern adaptation
Why: Disproportionate loss of automatic control relative to voluntary control.

R_poincare_SD1, R_poincare_SD2, R_poincare_SD_ratio
----------------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot Poincaré features
PD Distinction: Same patterns, with potential asymmetry in early PD

L_sample_entropy
----------------
Description: Measures regularity/complexity of stride interval series
Normal: Moderate values (1.0-2.0) indicate healthy complexity
PD Distinction: DECREASED values indicate:
- More regular, predictable stride patterns
- Loss of natural gait complexity
- Overly stereotyped movement
Why: Healthy gait has inherent complexity from multiple neural systems interacting.
     PD simplifies this to more rigid, less adaptive patterns.

R_sample_entropy
----------------
Description: Right foot stride regularity
PD Distinction: Same decrease as left side

L_rqa_determinism (Recurrence Quantification Analysis)
------------------------------------------------------
Description: Percentage of recurrent, predictable patterns in gait
Normal: Moderate determinism reflects structured variability
PD Distinction: DECREASED determinism indicates:
- Less predictable gait patterns
- Increased randomness in stepping
- Loss of motor program consistency
Why: Disruption of stable motor programs in basal ganglia-thalamocortical circuits.

L_rqa_avg_diagonal
------------------
Description: Average length of predictable sequences
PD Distinction: SHORTER sequences indicate:
- Brief periods of stability interrupted by irregularities
- Inability to maintain consistent rhythm
Why: Intermittent breakdown of motor control due to dopamine deficiency.

L_rqa_max_diagonal
------------------
Description: Maximum length of predictable sequences
PD Distinction: SHORTER maximum sequences indicate:
- Limited capacity for sustained rhythmic walking
- Frequent gait disruptions
Why: Reduced stability of motor output over time.

L_rqa_entropy
-------------
Description: Complexity of recurrence patterns
PD Distinction: DECREASED entropy indicates:
- Simpler, less complex dynamic structure
- Reduced adaptive capacity
Why: Loss of multifactorial neural control integration.

R_rqa_determinism, R_rqa_avg_diagonal, R_rqa_max_diagonal, R_rqa_entropy
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot RQA features
PD Distinction: Same patterns with potential asymmetry

================================================================================
3. ENHANCED VARIABILITY FEATURES
================================================================================

L_consecutive_variability
-------------------------
Description: Average absolute difference between consecutive strides
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Unstable, erratic stepping
- Poor step-to-step consistency
- Freezing or festination episodes
Why: Impaired internal cueing and rhythm generation.

L_consecutive_var_cv
--------------------
Description: Variability of consecutive differences
PD Distinction: INCREASED CV indicates:
- Inconsistent step-to-step adjustments
- Unpredictable gait pattern changes
Why: Fluctuating dopamine levels and motor control instability.

R_consecutive_variability, R_consecutive_var_cv
-----------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot consecutive variability
PD Distinction: Same increases, potential asymmetry

L_mean_asymmetry
----------------
Description: Average asymmetry between consecutive strides
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:
- Uneven step timing
- Irregular rhythm
- Uncoordinated lower limb movement
Why: Differential impairment of left vs right motor pathways.

L_max_asymmetry
---------------
Description: Maximum asymmetry in stride series
PD Distinction: INCREASED maximum asymmetry indicates:
- Occasional severe timing disruptions
- Episodic gait breakdown
- Freezing episodes
Why: Periodic complete loss of motor coordination.

R_mean_asymmetry, R_max_asymmetry
---------------------------------
Description: Right foot asymmetry measures
PD Distinction: Same patterns

4. GAIT PHASE FEATURES
================================================================================

L_stance_phase_mean
-------------------
Description: Average left foot ground contact time
Normal: 60-64% of gait cycle
PD Distinction: INCREASED stance time due to:
- Prolonged double support for stability
- Reduced confidence in single-leg support
- Slower weight transfer
Why: Compensatory strategy for postural instability and balance deficits.

L_stance_phase_std
------------------
Description: Variability in stance duration
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Inconsistent foot placement
- Unreliable weight acceptance
- Balance corrections
Why: Unpredictable motor output and balance control.

L_stance_phase_cv
-----------------
Description: Coefficient of variation for stance time
PD Distinction: INCREASED CV indicates proportional instability
Why: Greater relative inconsistency in fundamental gait phase.

R_stance_phase_mean, R_stance_phase_std, R_stance_phase_cv
----------------------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot stance features
PD Distinction: Same patterns, potential asymmetry

L_swing_phase_mean
------------------
Description: Average left foot aerial phase time
Normal: 36-40% of gait cycle
PD Distinction: DECREASED swing time due to:
- Shuffling gait (reduced foot clearance)
- Festination (hurried steps)
- Reduced step length
Why: Difficulty with leg elevation and forward propulsion.

L_swing_phase_std
-----------------
Description: Variability in swing duration
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Inconsistent leg advancement
- Variable foot clearance
- Unreliable stepping
Why: Impaired motor coordination during dynamic phase.

L_swing_phase_cv
----------------
Description: Coefficient of variation for swing time
PD Distinction: INCREASED CV indicates proportional inconsistency
Why: Unreliable execution of most dynamic gait phase.

R_swing_phase_mean, R_swing_phase_std, R_swing_phase_cv
--------------------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot swing features
PD Distinction: Same patterns

L_double_support_mean
---------------------
Description: Average time both feet are on ground
Normal: 10-20% of gait cycle
PD Distinction: INCREASED double support time due to:
- Enhanced stability strategy
- Reduced single-leg balance confidence
- Slower weight transfers
Why: Compensatory mechanism for postural instability and fear of falling.

L_double_support_std
--------------------
Description: Variability in double support time
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Inconsistent balance strategy
- Variable weight transfer timing
- Unpredictable stability adjustments
Why: Fluctuating postural control and confidence.

R_double_support_mean, R_double_support_std
--------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot double support features
PD Distinction: Same patterns

L_stance_ratio
--------------
Description: Stance time as proportion of total gait cycle
PD Distinction: INCREASED ratio (>65%) indicates:
- Disproportionate time spent in stable double support
- Reduced time in risky single support
- Conservative gait strategy
Why: Adaptive response to balance impairment and fall risk.

L_swing_ratio
-------------
Description: Swing time as proportion of total cycle
PD Distinction: DECREASED ratio (<35%) indicates:
- Reduced dynamic phase
- Conservative stepping pattern
- Shuffling gait characteristics
Why: Compensation for reduced balance and propulsion.

R_stance_ratio, R_swing_ratio
-----------------------------
Description: Right foot phase ratios
PD Distinction: Same patterns

5. FORCE PRODUCTION FEATURES
================================================================================

L_peak_force_mean
-----------------
Description: Average maximum force during left steps
PD Distinction: VARIABLE changes - may be increased or decreased:
- Increased: Stiff-legged gait with hard heel strikes
- Decreased: Weak push-off and reduced propulsion
Why: Altered muscle tone and force modulation.

L_peak_force_std
----------------
Description: Variability in peak forces
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Inconsistent force application
- Unreliable heel strikes
- Variable push-off strength
Why: Poor force control due to basal ganglia dysfunction.

L_peak_force_cv
---------------
Description: Coefficient of variation for peak forces
PD Distinction: INCREASED CV indicates proportional inconsistency
Why: Unreliable force generation relative to average.

R_peak_force_mean, R_peak_force_std, R_peak_force_cv
-----------------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot peak force features
PD Distinction: Same patterns, potential asymmetry

L_impulse_mean
--------------
Description: Average total force impulse (area under force curve)
PD Distinction: DECREASED impulse indicates:
- Reduced total force production
- Weaker push-off
- Shorter force application time
Why: Bradykinesia and reduced muscle activation.

L_impulse_std
-------------
Description: Variability in force impulse
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Inconsistent force generation
- Variable push-off strength
- Unreliable propulsion
Why: Fluctuating motor unit recruitment.

L_impulse_cv
------------
Description: Coefficient of variation for impulse
PD Distinction: INCREASED CV indicates proportional inconsistency
Why: Unreliable total force output.

R_impulse_mean, R_impulse_std, R_impulse_cv
--------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot impulse features
PD Distinction: Same patterns

6. SIGNAL QUALITY & SMOOTHNESS
================================================================================

L_jerk_mean
-----------
Description: Average rate of force change (movement smoothness)
PD Distinction: INCREASED jerk indicates:
- Less smooth force application
- Jerky movements
- Poor force control
Why: Discontinuous muscle activation patterns.

L_jerk_std
----------
Description: Variability in jerk
PD Distinction: INCREASED variability indicates:
- Inconsistent movement smoothness
- Variable force control
- Unpredictable movement quality
Why: Fluctuating motor control quality.

R_jerk_mean, R_jerk_std
-----------------------
Description: Right foot jerk features
PD Distinction: Same patterns

L_dominant_frequency
--------------------
Description: Main frequency component in left gait
PD Distinction: OFTEN INCREASED frequency due to:
- Shorter, quicker steps (festination)
- Hurried gait pattern
- Reduced step length
Why: Compensatory strategy for instability.

L_spectral_entropy
------------------
Description: Complexity of force frequency spectrum
PD Distinction: DECREASED entropy indicates:
- Simpler frequency composition
- Less complex motor output
- More stereotyped movement
Why: Reduced neural integration complexity.

L_gait_power_ratio
------------------
Description: Proportion of power in gait frequency band (0.3-3.0 Hz)
PD Distinction: ALTERED ratio may indicate:
- Different frequency distribution
- Changed harmonic content
- Altered movement rhythm
Why: Modified neural oscillatory patterns.

R_dominant_frequency, R_spectral_entropy, R_gait_power_ratio
-------------------------------------------------------------
Description: Right foot spectral features
PD Distinction: Same patterns

7. BILATERAL ASYMMETRY FEATURES
================================================================================

asymm_stride_time_mean
----------------------
Description: Left-right difference in average stride time
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry in early-stage PD due to:
- Hemiparkinsonism (one-sided symptoms)
- Uneven step timing
- Asymmetric motor impairment
Why: Unilateral dopamine depletion in early disease stages.

asymm_stride_time_cv
--------------------
Description: Left-right difference in stride time variability
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:
- Different variability patterns between sides
- Uneven rhythm disruption
- Asymmetric motor control impairment
Why: Differential basal ganglia involvement.

asymm_dfa_alpha
---------------
Description: Left-right difference in fractal scaling
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:
- Different complexity loss between sides
- Uneven dynamic impairment
- Asymmetric neural control disruption
Why: Unilateral disruption of complex gait control.

asymm_poincare_SD1
------------------
Description: Left-right difference in short-term variability
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:
- Different automatic control impairment
- Uneven step-to-step adjustment capacity
- Asymmetric gait automaticity loss
Why: Differential impairment of automatic movement control.

asymm_poincare_SD2
------------------
Description: Left-right difference in long-term variability
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:
- Different adaptive capacity between sides
- Uneven pattern variability
- Asymmetric long-term control
Why: Differential involvement of adaptive control systems.

asymm_sample_entropy
--------------------
Description: Left-right difference in gait regularity
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:
- Different complexity patterns
- Uneven regularity changes
- Asymmetric dynamic structure
Why: Differential complexity disruption.

asymm_stance_phase_mean
-----------------------
Description: Left-right difference in stance time
PD Distinction: INCREASED asymmetry indicates:

asymm_swing_phase_mean

asymm_stance_ratio

asymm_peak_force_mean

These features capture the fundamental neural control deficits in Parkinson's:
- Loss of automatic movement control
- Reduced complexity and adaptability  
- Increased variability and instability
- Compensatory strategies for balance
- Asymmetric motor impairment

The combination of temporal, dynamic, and bilateral features provides a
comprehensive quantification of Parkinsonian gait disturbances.
